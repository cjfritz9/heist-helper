# Changelog

## 0.13.0 (unreleased)

- **Minimap shows your section's loot, on every level.** Loot was filtered by height (hidden more than 400 units above or below you), but sections 1 and 2 alone have loot at three heights (about 4,930, 2,940 and 256), so that hid half your section and showed other sections' loot. The minimap now shows what's left in the section you're in, the same set as the panel's section counts, at full strength whatever its height. Ghosts are shown everywhere.
- **Minimap icon styles**, cycled with **Minimap icons: …** in ⚙ and remembered in `minimap.csv`: *pixel* (the shaded pixel-art icons), *native* (the default: painted pictures of the vault's own objects in the style of RS3's map icons: a dark wood strongbox with iron bands and a gold lock, the rare chest as a purple strongbox with lilac sparkles, the gunmetal safe with its white dial, a skull and crossbones in orange for corpses, and spirit's hooded spectre for ghosts, red or amber), *digsite* (Kharid-et Archaeology find-tags: the object inked in sepia on parchment over a wash of its colour, tied with matching string) and *spirit* (an open chest of coins, a gem chest, a vault door, a tombstone, a hooded spectre) and *mixed* (spirit, with the native safe in a soft cyan glow and a skull and crossbones for corpses). The last four are PNGs in `icons/<set>/` loaded with `bolt.createsurfacefrompng`, drawn at 4× and scaled down by `tools/make_icons.py` (Python with Pillow, dev only); they can be edited in any image editor, and missing files fall back to pixel. Several other styles were tried and dropped. Preview: `docs/minimap-icons.png`.
- **Minimap icons.** Loot left on the minimap is drawn as small pixel-art icons instead of squares: a chest, a shadow chest with a sparkle, a safe with its dial, a skull for corpses. Ghosts are a ghost icon instead of the diamond, red when synced and amber when estimated. Each is 22×22 px (11×11 pixel art, doubled; `ICON_SCALE` in `game/minimap.lua`), in the same colour as its outline in the world (`core/minimapicons.lua`).
- **⚙ Keep patrols drawn out of view** (off by default). Once a patrol is synced, its outline and minimap diamond carry on along its route by tick count after the ghost leaves view, placed one tile ahead of where the ghost would be drawn, and hand straight back to the seen ghost when it reappears. The routes are fixed and fully known, but this is the case the design report's compliance section names ("patrol prediction beyond what a player watching the screen can see"), so it's a separate setting, to be turned off or removed before submitting to the official API.
- **Ghosts on the minimap.** Each ghost the plugin is drawing gets a diamond on the minimap, bigger than the loot dots and edged in black, on the same tile as its outline: red for a synced patrol's true tile or a corpse spawn, amber for an estimate. Only ghosts currently in view are shown, never ones predicted from their route out of sight. Shown when the ghosts setting is on.
- **Crystal clicks recognised from what the game shows.** RS3 draws a cross where you click: a small cross growing outward when the click interacts with something (crystal, barrier), a shrinking X when it's a walk. And with mouseover text on, the target's name is drawn by the cursor in cyan. A left click that brings the interaction cross, with "Shadow crystal" under the cursor at that moment, now starts the maze for the nearest shadow crystal in view, whatever the crystal's outline on screen says. Without mouseover text, the interaction cross next to a crystal's position box counts. Learned with **Probe clicks**; the letter and cross shapes are pixel hashes at the user's interface scale (`data/clicktargets.lua`, `core/clicktarget.lua`, `game/clicks.lua`). The outline and position-box checks stay as backups, and a crystal can't be triggered twice within 1.5 s.
- Dev panel, **Probe clicks** (Map objects): for 15 s (press again to stop early), records every click and the interface images drawn around the mouse (200 px) to `clickprobe.log`, with bitmaps. It's for learning RS3's click cross (red on an object, yellow on a walk) and the mouseover text, so a crystal click can be recognised from what the game shows rather than from the crystal's shape on screen. The XP-drop probe now shares the same code (`game/imageprobe.lua`).
- **Crystal clicks that missed: also matched by the crystal's position.** In a run the east crystal (13,−78) was recognised but three clicks on it at the same spot weren't inside its outline, so the maze never started. The outline comes from the first time the crystal's model is drawn each frame, which may not be the main camera's pass. A click now also counts when it lands on a box around the crystal's tile projected with the main camera (900 units tall, at least 25 px either side), logged as "crystal click caught by its position". Misses log both boxes, to see which was off.
- **Leaving the vault before finishing resets the run.** Only the "Completion Time" line followed by the teleport out reset it, so leaving early (after a catch, near the start) kept the old run: its corpses, looted chests and a −4 section split showed up in the next one. Being seen in the entrance area (`core/lobby.lua`) with anything recorded in the run now resets it, logged in `loot.log` as "left the vault before finishing".
- **Fixed: patrol outlines pushed up to two tiles ahead.** Until a patrol is confirmed by a set-off after a stall (sections 1–2 often never are while in view), its sync is re-anchored on every tile the ghost walks onto. The guard that stops the outline stepping backwards compared tick counts from the old anchor with counts from the new one, so it held the outline one or two tiles further on. It now compares absolute ticks. Replaying a section 1–2 ghost from the log, the outline is now one tile ahead of the ring on every tile, where it had been two on many of them.
- **Patrol and spawn vision tiles are much easier to see (on the heavy side, to tone down from).** Outlines are twice as thick (6 px), and the tiles a ghost can see are filled with translucent red. Amber estimates stay as thin outlines.
- `loot.log` notes a left click that lands near a shadow crystal in view (within 8 tiles of you) but outside it, with the click's position. In one run the east → south maze never started: no crystal click was recognised at all, and nothing was logged about why.
- **Patrol outlines one tile ahead of the ghost, everywhere.** They were drawn a fixed half tick behind the route timing, but how far the drawn ghost trails its true tile isn't fixed: about 1.3 ticks just after a stall or turn, growing to about 2.4 along a straight. So the outline led the ghost by about a tile after turns (right) and two tiles on straights (wrong, screenshot from section 4). The outline is now placed one tick after the tile the ghost is drawn on, along its route, and never past where the sync puts the server: one tile ahead while it walks; on its stall tile while it stands; the next tile once the server has moved on. Replaces `DRAW_OFFSET_TICKS`.
- **Patrol syncs keep being checked while the ghost walks.** Once a patrol had been confirmed by a set-off, walking was ignored, so a sync that later slipped a whole tick stayed wrong until the next stall in view. Now every tile the ghost walks onto is compared with where the sync says it should be (using that tile's known drawn lag); three tiles in a row a whole tick off (to within 0.3 of a tick) move the sync by that tick, logged in `ghosts.log` as "walked 3 tiles 1 tick behind the sync". Small wobble never moves it. This doesn't change a sync that is consistent with the ghost's own walking: whether the outline itself is drawn a tick too far along is a separate question, under investigation.
- **Countdown on ghosts spawned by rummaging a corpse.** They stand for exactly 12 ticks and despawn, so they now show the ticks left as a bar over the ghost's head: 12 cells, one emptying each tick, counted on the shared tick clock from the tick the ghost appeared. Leftovers from leaving the instance, which never despawn, lose their bar once their 12 ticks are up (`core/spawntimer.lua`).
- **Maze true tile checked against your drawn character as you run.** Your character is drawn along exactly the tiles your true tile took, about a tick behind, so the plugin now keeps the true tile's path and checks each tile your character is drawn on against it. Drawn two or more tiles away from anywhere the true tile has been in the last 1.8 s means the true tile went wrong (a pathing detour, a click the game didn't follow): it starts again from where you're drawn. One tile off is allowed, since cutting a corner diagonally can round onto the side tile. Each leg also logs, per tile, how long after the true tile your character got there (or "not passed yet"), to learn what normal looks like; once that's known, a true tile running a tick early or late can be caught while you run, not just when you stop.
- **Maze true tile recovers from a stall.** In a leg with a stall, a click 87 ms before a tick missed it (so your round trip that session was over 87 ms, not the assumed 50), you stood a tick, and the plugin's true tile had already moved on. When it then saw you standing it snapped back to your drawn tile and dropped the step, so the highlight ran a tick behind for the rest of the straight. Now, standing still within a tick of a step means that click missed its tick: the true tile goes back to where you are, the click is kept and counted on the next tick (as the server does), and the round trip is raised to at least the click's margin, so the next close click is judged right. A click more than 200 ms before its tick that you didn't follow is taken as one the game ignored and dropped. Separately, the true tile no longer snaps back to your drawn tile in the first 300 ms after a step: after a standing click your character takes 40–100 ms to set off, and that gap used to cost the step.
- **Bag readings ignored for 10 s after logging in or the run starting.** Logging back in inside the vault, the bag's tooltip read 480 (the previous run's full bag) for a moment, and the counter took it; the real figure was 40. And a later bag reading that corrects an earlier one now takes the difference back out of the section the earlier one went into, not the section you're in: that wrong 480 went into section 1 and its correction into section 2, which showed −387/75.
- **Panel warns when chat can't be read.** Corpse rummages, catches and the run's end are read from chat; when the chat box is scrolled up or can't be found (5 s without a successful read), the panel now says so under the section counts, and `loot.log` records when chat stops and starts being readable. It stopped silently after a login: chests and safes kept counting (they're seen), corpses didn't.
- **Maze highlight was a tick behind a tick-perfect run: fixed.** A test leg clicked once per tick showed 5 of 9 clicks landing "1 ahead of the highlighted one". Cause: whenever a frame showed your character in the same spot as the frame before (which happens constantly, the game moves it less often than frames are drawn), the plugin took you for standing and put the true tile back on your drawn tile, which trails the real one by 2–3 tiles while running. Every click then needed an extra tick to "arrive". Standing now means not having moved for 150 ms, so the true tile holds its lead between clicks. Also, every left click on a tile near the maze now moves the true tile, not only clicks on highlighted steps: the click you make before the route appears, or one off the route, no longer leaves the model behind. The route advances whenever the true tile lands on one of its steps. A true tile more than 6 tiles from your drawn character is taken as wrong and pulled back. Replaying the test leg's clicks through the new model, every click is on the highlighted step.
- **Maze true tile rebuilt** (`core/truetile.lua`). The log of a run where it felt off showed two faults. (1) Your round trip was guessed from how long clicks made while standing took to start you moving, which is mostly the wait for the next tick: it came out at 300–700 ms, so every click was counted a tick or more late. (2) Each new click threw away the one before it, so clicking the next tile before the previous click's tick had come meant that step never registered, and with the late counting that was nearly every click: the route only advanced when your drawn character arrived, which is about a tick behind. Now every click is kept with the time it reaches the server, and on each tick of the shared clock the true tile steps towards the latest click that had arrived by then, so clicking ahead no longer cancels the step already on its way. The round trip starts at 50 ms and is narrowed from standing clicks on a highlighted step that only just made or missed a tick. Other standing clicks aren't used: clicks on crystals and other objects set you off late for reasons that have nothing to do with ping, and one of those had pushed the figure up to 81 ms. Setting off less than 15 ms after a click is taken as caused by something else, and samples that contradict each other (a later run had "at least 131 ms" and "at most 19 ms") fall back to 50 ms instead of being averaged. A click made while standing steps on the tick without waiting to see you move, unless it's within 40 ms of being too late for that tick, when it waits to see which tick you set off on. `loot.log` records every left click while a route is showing: the tile clicked (on the route or not), whether that was the highlighted step or how many steps ahead of or behind it, the true tile and your drawn tile at that moment, and how long before a tick. It also records every set-off, and when the route advanced because your drawn character arrived rather than the true tile. So a maze run tick-perfect shows exactly where the highlight and your clicks part ways.
- **Tick clock: read from XP drops, then left running.** Replaying 11 sessions of `ticks.log` showed what each source is worth. XP drops land within about 16 ms of the tick (except the first one after a pause of half a minute or more, which comes 30–80 ms late). Your own movement starts are seen about 37 ms after the tick with a long tail, ghosts' about 53 ms. And the tick runs about 600.055 ms on this machine's clock, sliding 5–6 ms later a minute, steadily: a clock set from XP drops and left alone was still within 10 ms after half an hour. So the clock is now set by XP drops alone (the last 2 minutes of them, following the slide), every drop re-syncs it, and between drops it keeps ticking by itself for up to 10 minutes. Movement starts and ghost set-offs are only used (corrected for their lateness) when there's been no XP drop for 10 minutes, or when five in a row agree with each other that the beat has moved (a world hop). Re-locking now also needs the off-beat samples to agree with each other, so scattered strays can't move the clock. Replayed over the logs, an XP drop lands a median 8 ms from where the clock expected it, 90% within 22 ms, including after 2 minutes or more without one.
- Dev panel, **Entrance area:** re-mark where the panel stays open between runs. Press **Corner A** and middle-click a tile, then **Corner B** and middle-click the opposite corner; the tiles don't have to be reachable, only on screen, on your floor and within 40 tiles of you. The rectangle between them is saved to `entrance.csv` and replaces the bundled area. With dev tools open the area in use is outlined in blue on the ground. **Keep panel open everywhere** keeps the panel (and so the dev tools) visible anywhere. **Clear** goes back to the bundled area.

- **Panel at the entrance:** the panel now stays open in the vault entrance area (tiles 2487–2495, 7577–7584, marked in game and bundled in `core/lobby.lua`), showing "Entrance", the last run's time and loot, and the run stats line. Outlines, scanning, chat reading and everything else stay off outside the vault. `panel.log` records your tile whenever the panel is shown or hidden, and `loot.log` the tile you land on when you're teleported out after a run.
- Cold-start blank panel, second attempt. The first panel window created after the plugin loads has come up blank on every first launch of the day. Now: the page reports when it has actually drawn a frame (two animation frames after its first state), and a page that's ready but never draws is reopened; and the first panel after the plugin loads is closed and recreated once, 1.5 s after it reports ready, whether or not it looks fine. `panel.log` records every open, ready, drawn frame and reopen with timings, so a cold start that still fails shows where it got stuck.

## 0.12.1

- **Minimap loot dots:** every chest, safe, rare chest and corpse still left to loot (the same ones the panel counts, so level toggles and crevices apply) is a dot on the minimap in its outline colour. Positioned from Bolt's minimap events: the minimap's place on screen, its rotation and zoom, and the world position it's centred on. Its terrain is 8 pixels a tile before zoom (64 world units a pixel, from Bolt's source), so dots stay on their tiles as you zoom or turn. Dots near the edge are left out so they don't spill off the round minimap. `game/minimap.lua`; confirmed in game to stay on their tiles through zooming and turning.

- **Run stats:** every completed run is recorded in `runs.csv` (completion time and loot) when the "Completion Time" line appears. The panel shows runs this session (since the plugin loaded) and in total, and this session's average and best time. More columns (gp, uniques, pilfer points) can be added to the same log.

- Synced ghost outlines are drawn half a tick later than the route timing (`DRAW_OFFSET_TICKS = -0.5` in `game/visionrings.lua`): at 0 the outline looked a tick ahead (standing in it for a tick didn't get the user caught), at −1 a tick behind. To settle it from data, every tick you stand within 8 tiles of a synced ghost's true tile and inside its tiles under any candidate offset (0, −½, −1), `ghosts.log` records which offsets had you inside; catches record the same. A few deliberate edge tests show which offset the game agrees with. Catches now use where you stood before any teleport (a catch in section 2 sends you to the arrival tile, and catches were logged as "you on 0,0").

- Added corpse 5,−18's spawn tile (9,−19, section 2) to `data/ghostspawns.lua`; 7 of the 8 corpses' spawn tiles are known now (11,−31's hasn't been seen). It's 4 tiles from its corpse, one more than the rummage rule allowed, so the rule now takes spawns within 4 tiles. Unrecognised, a leftover spawn there wasn't pinned, and section 1–2's patrol walking past on x = 8 swapped identities with it: the patrol's tracking stuck on the spawn's tile and the spawn's outline went missing. Known spawn tiles are pinned the moment they appear, including leftovers from the bug.

- Maze true tile on the shared tick clock, with your ping taken into account. A click made while moving used to count for the very next tick, but the server only acts on a click that reaches it before its tick, so a click shortly before a tick landed a tick later and the maze showed you a step ahead. Now the plugin measures your round trip from clicks made while standing (the time until you start moving; a low percentile of the last 12, `core/latency.lua`), and a click counts for the first tick it can reach. Tick boundaries come from the shared tick clock (XP drops and set-offs) instead of your movement starts alone.

- **Ghost patrols sync within about a second of coming into view.** The drawn ghost lags its server tile by a different amount at each tile of its route (1.3–2.9 ticks), but by the same amount every lap: repeatable to about 0.03 ticks per tile over hundreds of passes. `tools/ghostroutes.lua` now learns each tile's lag from the recordings, anchored to set-offs, and bundles it in `data/ghostpaths.lua`. Each tile a ghost is seen reaching then gives its server timing (arrival time minus that tile's lag); once 2 tiles agree within 0.2 ticks the patrol is synced, rounded onto an exact tick by the tick clock (XP drops and set-offs), and turns red. Set-offs after a stall, which land on the exact tick, are checkpoints: the sync is checked there and pulled onto the exact tick if it's off, after which walking no longer moves it. Replaying the recordings: synced 1.0–1.4 s after a ghost came into view on average, and the first set-off afterwards agreed 22 of 23 times (the other was a tick off and got tightened). This replaces waiting for stalls and the "left a wait" quick sync. A single tile that can't be placed on its own (a ghost's first tile in view) is kept and used once the next one places it.
- The route tool puts back corners that a sighting cut: a lone diagonal step where other sightings show an L-turn through a corner tile becomes that turn. One such pass had stopped section 4's loop from closing.

- Fixed: section 4's patrol could stay amber for minutes. Finding where a ghost is on its loop needed its last 4 tiles, and section 4's ghost often came into view just 2 tiles before a stall, followed by its longest stretch without waits (about 94 s). A ghost is now located from the fewest recent tiles that match only one place, down to 2; all 641 steps of the four loops are told apart by their last 2 tiles alone.

- Ghost outline colours: **red** means the plugin knows the ghost's true tile (a patrol synced to its route, or a static spawn), **amber** means it's estimated (a patrol not synced yet). Static spawns are now red: they never move, so their tile is their true tile.
- The six known spawn tiles are bundled in `data/ghostspawns.lua` (one per corpse, each seen 6–8 times on exactly the same tile; the two section 2 corpses haven't spawned one yet). A ghost appearing on one is a spawn at once, instead of after 5 s without moving.
- Patrols turn red sooner: reaching the first tile after a wait gives a quick provisional sync (the drawn ghost has caught up while the server made it wait, so it moved there a tick before reaching the centre). Only a set-off after a real stall, which lands on the exact server tick, confirms it; if it disagrees it corrects it once, and a second set-off confirms. Quick syncs alone were sometimes a tick late, so they never confirm each other. Replays: 46 of 47 set-offs after syncing on the predicted tick.
- Amber outlines follow the route when the ghost's loop is known from its last 4 tiles (the next tile along the loop while it moves), so they no longer overshoot turns.

- Fixed flicker on synced ghost outlines: the tick clock refines its phase with each new sample, which near a tick change could move the shown tile forward, back and forward again for a few frames; the shown tick of a patrol now never steps back. Outlines also stay drawn for 250 ms after the ring was last seen, so a frame where the game skips drawing it doesn't blink the outline out.

- **Ghost routes pinned:** all four patrols are closed loops, bundled in `data/ghostpaths.lua`: section 4 (305 steps, 328 ticks a lap), section 3 south (114, 129), section 3 north (94, 116), sections 1–2 (128, 155). Lap lengths are measured directly from the time between passes of the same tile on consecutive laps (653 of 653 agree for section 4). Stalls come from when the ghost arrives at each tile within its lap, not from spotting it standing still: ghosts also pause on turnarounds without the client ever showing them still, 15–27 hidden ticks a lap. Replayed against the recordings, tile times match the model exactly 71–93% of the time and within a tick for nearly all the rest.
- **Ghost true tile from the route, synced once:** a patrol is synced from the moment its ghost sets off after a real stall (still at least 150 ms), which happens on the exact server tick. A second set-off must agree before it locks in; if it doesn't, the second becomes the new candidate. From then on the ghost's true tile comes purely from counting ticks since that moment (rounded to whole 0.6 s ticks, so small wobbles in the tick clock can't shift it) along its loop. Nothing it does later moves the sync; later set-offs are only checked and logged in `ghosts.log` when they disagree. A ghost walking into view is picked up from its loop's synced position (within 2 tiles) or its last 4 tiles. Synced ghosts are outlined in red, others in amber on the estimate. Replaying the three recorded runs, 46 of 47 set-offs after syncing landed on exactly the predicted tick, up to 10 minutes after a single sync.
- Route timing is modelled on the server, not the client: three of the ghosts are drawn walking slower than a tile per tick (0.64–0.66 s a tile; section 4's keeps up at 0.60), so the drawn ghost falls behind its true tile along straights and catches up while the server makes it wait at turns. `tools/ghostroutes.lua` only uses the drawn ghost for the path; waits sit on turns and stall tiles, measured turn to turn, pinned exactly by the tick counts between consecutive set-offs, and made to add up to the lap length measured from whole laps.
- `tools/ghostroutes.lua <logs…> [--write]` measures lap lengths, places stalls by arrival time, checks every tile time against the model, and with `--write` produces `data/ghostpaths.lua`. Missed tile centres (a two-tile straight or diagonal jump) are filled in; a lap closes on a 4-tile overlap.

- Each run's ghost recording is also saved to its own file, `ghosts-run-N.log` (numbered in `ghostruns.csv`), so sightings from several runs can be stitched together; `ghosts.log` only holds about one run. `tools/ghostroutes.lua` takes any number of these logs plus `ghostroutes.csv`. The first centre-recorded run is kept in `mapping/ghosts-centre-run-1.log`. It showed the corner at −25,−18 is a real L-turn (two steps a tick apart), not the diagonal the edge-based recording had merged it into.

- Ghost tiles are now recorded when a ghost passes through a tile's centre (within 0.3 of a tile, at the frame it's closest; the client rounds corners at turns, seen coming only 0.12–0.13 from the centre, while a real diagonal never comes within 0.7 of a neighbouring centre), not when it crosses a tile edge. Each tick's step ends at a tile centre, so every step is caught once and a diagonal step never records the corner tiles it clips.
- Ghost vision tiles are drawn around each ghost's true tile: while it slides between tiles, the tile it's heading to (the server has already moved it there); while it stands, its own tile. Spawns stay on their drawn tile. Catches also log the true tile and whether you were inside its tiles. This is a stopgap until routes are pinned and the true tile comes from the route and the tick clock.
- `tools/ghostroutes.lua <ghosts.log> [ghostroutes.csv]`: stitches each ghost sighting in `ghosts.log` (spawns skipped, corner detours from older recordings removed) into routes by overlaps of at least 8 tiles (`core/routeassembly.lua`), closes loops, and prints each route with its stalls and ticks a lap. First result: both section 3 patrols are closed loops (114 steps + 4 stall ticks = 118 ticks; 94 steps + 9 = 103).

- Tick clock: XP drops are now a trusted source. The probe showed one drop per tick while cracking a safe (gaps 0.578–0.622 s, averaging 0.598) landing at the same point in the tick as movement starts (within about 10 ms). A drop is recognised by its orange "+" (8×9 pixels, hash `f39c97d3`, colour #f5b241; the black shadow copy is ignored) newly appearing, followed as it scrolls so each drop counts once. The hash is specific to the user's interface scale; other players would need it found automatically. Loot popups ("N Loot Gained") are measured against the clock as a candidate, from the frame each popup is first seen, to check whether their slide-in delays them.

- Dev panel, **Probe XP drops** (in Map objects): press it, middle-click where your XP drops appear, then crack a safe or pickpocket. For 20 s it records every interface image (up to 128 px) that appears within 150 px of the click to `xpprobe.log`: its pixel hash, size, colour and position, a bitmap the first time each is seen, and how far from the tick clock's predicted tick it appeared. Each image is followed as it moves, so a drop scrolling away counts once. This is to find what XP drops look like and whether they land on the tick, before using them for the tick clock.

- Fixed: the run was reset as soon as the "Completion Time" chat line appeared, while you were still in the vault. Everything still in view then leaked into the empty run: opened chests were marked again, and hovering the bag set the finished run's 480 back into it. The game only resets when it teleports you out to the entrance, so the plugin now waits for that: the completion line only marks the run finished, and the reset happens on the first frame after you've left the vault.

- Ghosts spawned by rummaging a corpse are kept out of route mapping and the tick clock (they're still drawn, since they can catch you). A ghost counts as a spawn when it appears within 3 tiles of a corpse rummaged in the last 6 s, when a rummage of a corpse next to it arrives within 1 s of it appearing (the ghost can appear just before that rummage's chat line: seen 87 ms before), or when it hasn't moved at all in the 5 s since appearing (spawns never move; the longest patrol stall seen is 3.8 s). A spawn is pinned to its tile, so a patrol walking past can't be mistaken for it and turn into fake route moves. `ghosts.log` labels spawns.

- Tick clock, measuring only for now (`core/tickphase.lua`, `game/ticksync.lua`): works out where the game's 0.6 s ticks fall on our clock. Only sources expected to share the same timing feed it: you and ghosts starting to move after standing still at least 150 ms. Its phase is the average position within the 0.6 s cycle of the last 30 s of samples (samples from slow frames count for less, for example when tabbed out). Three samples in a row more than 100 ms off make it lock again from fresh samples. New chat lines are measured against it but don't feed it, to see whether they land at the same point in the tick. `ticks.log` records every sample with its source and distance from the predicted tick, plus a per-source summary every 30 s. Nothing uses the clock yet; the maze keeps its own. XP drops are the next candidate, after a probe of their images.

- Ghost routes are pieced together in `ghostroutes.csv`, which is never trimmed and only gains information. Each line is a fact with a count of how often it's been seen: `move,fromHeight,fromX,fromZ,toHeight,toX,toZ,ticks` (the tick count is `-` for the first step after standing, whose timing isn't clean) and `stop,height,x,z,ticks` (only recorded when the stop began in view, so partial stops never count). A stall's length in ticks is its time divided by 0.6 s plus a sixth, rounded down: the client finishes each step's animation a little before the tick ends, so stalls measure 0.1–0.4 s longer than whole ticks (seen: 0.69–1.02 s for 1 tick, 1.93–2.21 s for 3, 3.78 s for 6). Pauses at turns under half a second come out as 0 ticks and aren't recorded. Heights keep routes on stacked floors apart and let routes that use stairs chain across floors: each height is matched to the nearest floor already seen within 100 units, or starts a new one. Moves back onto the same tile (a ghost flickering on a tile boundary) aren't recorded. Lines from before heights were added are moved to `ghostroutes-noheight.csv`, not mixed in. Times are rounded to 0.6 s ticks, and tile crossings less than half a tick apart are merged, so a diagonal step clipping a corner doesn't add a fake tile. No ghost names are stored: routes are static, so the moves chain into each loop by themselves. Saved at most every 5 s. `ghosts.log` stays as the detailed record to check it against.

- Ghost route recording, for mapping patrol routes and timings: every frame, each vision ring is tracked (kept as the same ghost by being the nearest ring within 2 tiles of where it was) and its moves are written to `ghosts.log`: when it appears, each tile it moves onto (with its exact position), when it starts moving and how long it stood still, when it stops and how long it moved, and when it's lost from view.
